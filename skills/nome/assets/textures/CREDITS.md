The original maps use NASA data and spacecraft imagery with artistic color adjustments and some reconstructed terrain. They are not all unmodified spacecraft photographs.

NOME modifications: the original files are unchanged. At runtime, the maps are projected onto spheres with limb shading; Earth clouds are composited over its surface. Additional directional shading and atmospheric glow are presentation effects.

Body sizes, orbital radii, inclinations and periods are composed for navigation. They are neither an astronomy simulation nor personal-profile scores. The six domain names remain the navigation labels.

## Ultraviolet sky background

File: ultraviolet-sky.webp (2400 × 1200, original WebP unchanged).
Author: Brice Ménard; map produced with Claude Science.
Article: https://www.anthropic.com/research/the-missing-map-of-the-sky
Original embedded image: `hero-uv` in https://assets.claude.ai/brand/artifacts/editorial/uvsky/uvmap_standalone.html
Map, downloads and licence: https://menard.pha.jhu.edu/uvmap/
Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0),
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Retrieved: 2026-10-20.

Credit: data from GALEX and Swift-UVOT (NASA), FIMS/SPEAR (KASI), TD-1 (ESRO),
Planck and Gaia (ESA). Map citation: Ménard, B. (2026), Full-sky ultraviolet maps
from harmonised GALEX, Swift-UVOT, FIMS/SPEAR, TD-1 and Gaia data, in preparation;
Murthy (2014, ApJS 213, 32) for the zero point.

This is a colour visualization of the far-UV/near-UV ratio in a Mollweide
projection. It combines measured data with predicted areas; it is not an
unmodified visible-light photograph. NOME uses it as a decorative background.

Presentation changes only: CSS overscan and viewport crop to hide the oval map
boundary, brightness and saturation adjustment, vignette and a dark reading-area
overlay. The image itself is unchanged and served locally, with no external
image request at runtime.
